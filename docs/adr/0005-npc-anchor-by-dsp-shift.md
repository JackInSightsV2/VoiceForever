# An NPC's anchor is its Archetype anchor, shifted by DSP

Every NPC needs its own anchor, and it must keep the character of the Archetype anchor a human picked (ADR-0004). We measured two ways to derive it on 10 orc_f and 10 human_m NPCs (#13), each NPC rendering two real lines as continuation from its anchor, scored with WavLM-SV speaker similarity, pitch and ASR.

VoxCPM2 voice design from the Archetype description plus seeded traits gives clearly different NPCs: between-NPC similarity was 0.79 (orc_f) and 0.83 (human_m), against 0.98 for one NPC's own lines. But it loses the character the human picked. For orc_f, 76 of 80 designs were more than 3 semitones above the approved 144 Hz anchor (median 233 Hz). Lines ended up +6.9 semitones higher on average and only 0.76 similar to the Archetype's own lines. For human_m, design mostly held (lines 0.88 similar, 7 of 10 NPCs cleared the gate).

Shifting the Archetype anchor itself with Praat (pitch ±0.8–2 semitones, formant, pace), then continuing from the shifted clip, keeps the character. Lines were 0.97 similar to the Archetype's own lines, HNR stayed within 0.6 dB, and line pitch followed the shift (r = 0.86–0.93). It costs no model render per NPC. Its NPCs are less distinct by speaker embedding: 0.95–0.96 between NPCs against 0.98 within one NPC. WavLM-SV barely registers pitch, and the formant shift drives most of that gap. So we use the DSP shift, with formant up to ±6% (±8% for named NPCs), and keep design behind `vo voices --strategy design`. The Neighbour floor (0.95 for now) is in DSP's range. It still has to be calibrated by ear on the Separation page, and if pitch-shifted Neighbours turn out to sound too alike, design is the fallback for plain-voiced Archetypes.
